### Threats

If a vulnerability is the weakness, a threat is something or someone that could take advantage of it. That includes:

- attackers
- malware
- insiders
- natural disasters
- nation-state groups

As that list shows, this category is wider than most people expect, because it covers anything that could cause the harm, whether or not there's any intent behind it. A flood that knocks out a data center is a threat in the same sense as a criminal group is, even though only one of them meant to be there.

What matters most about that list, though, is how little of it you control. You can't patch a criminal organization out of existence, you can't switch off a storm, and you can't completely rule out someone inside the business doing something they shouldn't. In practice, then, threats are a fixed part of the environment you work in, so you plan around them rather than expecting them to go away.

What you can control is:

- **the weakness they would have to use**
- **the damage they could do if they succeeded**

That's why threats and weaknesses matter together rather than on their own. The same threat will get very different results against a well-patched system than against a neglected one, and the thing that changes the outcome is the weakness. Since the weakness is the part you can do something about, that's where your effort pays off.

Knowing which threats are realistic for your own organization also helps, because it keeps that effort in proportion. A regional accounting firm and a defense contractor face genuinely different attackers, for example. Treat every organization as though a nation-state group were about to show up, and you'd spend effort far better aimed at what's likely to happen.
