### What a SOC Analyst Actually Does

A SOC runs around the clock, watching a stream of alerts generated by the firewalls, endpoint tools, authentication logs, and the SIEM that ties them together. A help desk technician you studied in Phase 1 fixes what a user reports. A system administrator configures and maintains the systems themselves. A SOC analyst does neither. Their job is to notice when something that was built and configured correctly starts behaving incorrectly, which is a different skill from building it. An analyst who has never seen a legitimate Kerberos ticket request has no basis for recognizing an abnormal one, which is why this phase comes after Active Directory and authentication, not before them.

Most SOCs split that watching into three tiers, and the split exists because triage, investigation, and hunting are different skills that do not all belong to the same person. A Tier 1 analyst works the incoming queue: real-time monitoring, first-pass triage, and the judgment call of what gets closed as benign versus escalated. A Tier 2 analyst takes what Tier 1 escalates and goes deeper, scoping what the account or host actually touched and building the fuller picture. A Tier 3 analyst works the other direction entirely. Instead of waiting on the queue, they hunt for attacker behavior nothing has flagged yet and build or tune the detection that should have caught it.

That tier split is also a career path, and the skill that changes most between tiers is not technical depth so much as scope. A Tier 1 analyst asks "does this one alert make sense." A Tier 3 analyst asks "what pattern is nobody looking for yet." Both questions use the same investigation mindset this week teaches. They just aim it at a different size of problem.
