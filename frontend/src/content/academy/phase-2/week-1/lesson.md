## Essential Question

What is malware?

## Overview

Read the material on this page to learn about malware and the current threats regarding malware.

## Malicious Software

Malware is a shortened term for malicious software.

Malware is unethical code hackers write to cause harm and destruction. Malware gains access to a system in myriad ways, usually without the consent or knowledge of the user. The most common vectors of this compute contaminant are portable storage devices and internet communications, such as email or social media.

Recent research has estimated the potential number of unique signatures (strings of code used to detect and identify specific malware) as into the billions. This is a massive increase since Symantec, a company that provides cybersecurity software and services around the world, noted more than 17 million unique signatures less than 10 years ago.

In network security, an attack vector is the hacker's path to exploit a network security vulnerability. All of the malware examples and insertion attacks are examples of attack vectors.

## Types of Malware

Most viruses infect files, as they initially need a host object to infect. Viruses spread through the actions of users. The virus will spread to other files when a user opens infected files. As infected files get sent to other systems, the virus spreads there.

Worms spread from system to system, unlike viruses, which spread from file to file. Worms do not require human interaction and can spread faster than viruses. Today most threats labeled as a virus tend to be a worm — hackers design worms around specific system flaws. Once the worm finds the specified flaw, it will exploit it to gain access to another system. Once it has access, the process repeats itself.

While not a type of malware, Trojan Horse is a malware distribution method. The Trojan Horse gets its name from the Trojan War, when the Greek army built a large hollow horse as a gift and hid their warriors inside. Once the Trojans brought the horse past their gates (think of network security), the Greeks emerged from inside the horse and attacked the Trojans. In today's world, someone can unknowingly download a trojan horse believing it was a game or a video, for example. Hackers can convert any program into a trojan by embedding the malware inside. Hackers use tools called wrappers to construct Trojans.

**Keystroke Loggers** records the keyboard activity of a user. Once a system is infected, the logger will send logs of keystrokes back to the hacker.

A **Phishing Attack** occurs when an email gets sent that appears legitimate or enticing. A phishing attack's purpose is to get the user to interact or click on the email and, thus, launch malware into the system.

**Spyware** is an advancement of keystroke logging to monitor and record other user activity. Spyware varies but can collect applications launched, websites visited, emails, chats, names of opened files, network activity, screen captures, and recordings from webcams and microphones.

**Adware** is malicious software that has infiltrated advertisements. Hackers will use spyware to create targeted adware for a user. Adware can be shown as a pop-up email or by replacing legitimate ads on a website.

**Rootkits** are malicious camouflage that functions as invisibility shields for anything a hacker wants to hide on a computer.

A **logic bomb** is an electronic "landmine." A logic bomb will remain dormant until a triggering event, such as a time/date, a program's launch, a keyword's typing, or even visiting a specific website. The triggering event launches the malware.

**Insertion attacks** introduce unauthorized content or devices to an otherwise secure infrastructure.

## Session Hi-Jacking, Spoofing, and Man-in-the-Middle Attacks

Attacks on systems and networks can involve falsification of credentials or misrepresentation. This collection of attacks consists of a hacker posing as another entity or sending messages that their system is a different machine.

**Session Hi-Jacking** occurs when a hacker can take over a connection after a client has authenticated with a server.

**Man-in-the-middle (MitM)** attacks occur when a hacker intervenes in a communication session between a client and a server. The attack usually involves fooling or tricking the client into initiating the session with the hacker's computer instead of with the intended server.

**Spoofing** is the falsification of information. Most spoofing is a falsification of the identity of a source. For example, email addresses, MAC addresses, and IP addresses are all easily spoofed. A hacker using a spoofing method tries tricking a user or a host into believing a communication originated from somewhere other than its source.
